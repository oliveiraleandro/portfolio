03 / Cervello BPMS

Cervello BPMS

What does a support team use every day in a ticketing platform?

  • Design System
  • Enterprise UX
  • HTML and CSS

Context and my role

In 2018, I joined Cervello, an IT and ticketing platform whose interface had accumulated controls and inconsistencies. For roughly four years I was its sole designer, conducting qualitative research, prototyping, developing the design system, and implementing designs in HTML, CSS, and SCSS.

The PM and I prioritized login, the home page, and ticket handling based on the support team’s most frequent tasks.

Consistent customization

Each client could use their own brand colors. I defined text and background pairings and generated variations in code. I kept the colors for success, warnings, and errors consistent so teams could recognize those signals in every theme.

I organized typography, spacing, and actions. For the customizable home page, I proposed a library of royalty-free images to simplify shortcut setup.

I also grouped fragmented screens into tabs. I proposed universal search to reach deeper pages, and the PM suggested breadcrumbs to help people stay oriented. We discussed these ideas with users in informal conversations using prototypes.

From visual patterns to code

Modules shared foundations, with their own controls where needed. With no dedicated front-end developer, I replaced much of Bootstrap with a BEM-based SCSS foundation that reused components and generated color options.

I worked within the existing JavaScript library for dashboards. In the app, I argued for giving home page configuration and dashboard creation lower priority. These were setup tasks that rarely came up in day-to-day support.

Working on both design and implementation let me carry patterns from the screens into the styles used across the platform’s modules.

I reviewed releases and kept the prototype in sync with the product.

Results and lessons learned

Users involved in testing, the PM, and the sales team gave positive feedback. Customization made it easier to present the platform to different clients.

We lacked systematic usage tracking. New contracts were signed, but I have no data linking them to the redesign.

The work produced a reorganized experience and reusable components. Today I’d use those lessons to contribute more to product direction.
