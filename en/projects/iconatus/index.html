04 / Iconatus · CAP

CAP on desktop and mobile

Information to support real estate agents in their daily work.

  • B2B Systems
  • Information Architecture
  • Responsive Design
  • Data Visualization

Context and my role

For three months, I was the sole designer on the CAP redesign, working with the founder and developers. The challenge was to bring the real estate platform to mobile while retaining its features. I spoke with two agents and mapped screens and user journeys.

What should agents see first on their phones?

Recent visits indicated activity on a property, but appeared in the last column of the table. On mobile, I moved visits to the top of each card, ahead of the summary and the link to details. I kept the table on desktop.

On the property detail screen, I proposed replacing tabs with a vertical layout. The client preferred tabs, and the alternative made for a long page. I adjusted tab states and navigation to avoid extra horizontal scrolling.

Based on the user journeys, I proposed highlighting recent property sourcing and follow-ups on the home page to help agents pick up where they left off. This was a hypothesis.

Pricing and trust

I emphasized current prices, plotted historical prices over time, and added regional comparisons. The client responded positively to the proposal.

One agent found the trends interesting but questioned the accuracy of the data. When I asked how to handle uncertainty, I was told that uncertain data would be excluded. Reliability also depended on decisions by the business and technology teams.

I narrowed the range of colors and typefaces and consolidated components after validating the desktop visual direction with the client.

Results and lessons learned

The redesign covered the entire platform, with reusable patterns and mobile-specific solutions. The parts of the implementation I saw closely matched the prototype.

There was no structured measurement after delivery. The results I observed were qualitative. With more time, I’d study actual usage and further explore the home page.

The project showed me that making data easier to understand can also surface questions an interface alone cannot resolve.
